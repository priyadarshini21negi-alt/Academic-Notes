- also known spike or nerve impulse 
- brief and rapid electrical signal that travels along the membrane of a neuron 
- It is fundamental means of communication between neurons and is responsible for transmitting information throughout nervous system.
- Action potentials are crucial for various physiological processes, including sensory perception, motor control, and cognitive functions like memory and learning.

- The generation and propagation of an action potential involve changes in the neuron's membrane potential, which is the difference in electrical charge between the inside and outside of the cell. The resting membrane potential of a neuron is usually negative, typically around **-70 millivolts (mV)**, maintained by the selective permeability of the cell membrane to different ions, particularly sodium (**Na+**), potassium (**K+**), and chloride (**Cl-**) ions.

# stages of an action potential process
### 1. Resting State 
- Neuron is at its resting membrane potential.
- has more -ve charge inside the cell compared to outside.
- Voltage gated (**Na+**)  - closed and voltage-gated potassium (**K+**) channels are mostly closed.

### 2.Depolarization 
When neuron receives a strong enough excitatory input, it depolarizes. 

**Depolarization** - process of membrane potential becoming less negative.
It occurs due to the opening voltage-gated sodium channels in response to the incoming positive charges.
Sodium ions rush into the cell, causing a rapid increase in the membrane potential.

### 3. Threshold 
 If the depolarization reaches a certain critical level, called the **threshold**, it triggers a positive feedback loop. At the threshold, a significant number of voltage-gated sodium channels open, leading to a massive influx of sodium ions, further depolarizing the membrane.

### 4. Rising Phase
The influx of sodium ions causes a sharp and rapid increase in the membrane potential. This positive feedback loop leads to the membrane potential rapidly reaching a peak value of around **+40 mV**.

### 5. Falling Phase
After reaching its peak, the voltage-gated sodium channels start to close, and voltage-gated potassium channels begin to open. The efflux of potassium ions from the cell leads to repolarization, restoring the negative membrane potential.

### 6. Undershoot/Afterhyperpolarization Phase
In some neurons, the repolarization phase can lead to a brief period of hyperpolarization, where the membrane potential becomes more negative than the resting potential. This is known as the afterhyperpolarization or undershoot. 

### 7. Refractory Period 
After an action potential, there is a refractory period during which the neuron is temporarily unable to generate another action potential. This period is essential to ensure that action potentials remain discrete and propagate in one direction along the neuron's axon.
