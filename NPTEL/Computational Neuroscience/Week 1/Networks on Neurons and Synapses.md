
# Role of synapses in facilitating these networks: 
## Synapse - 
The places where neurons connect and communicate with each other are called synapses.

They are specialized junctions that allow communication between neurons in the nervous system. These connections are essential for transmitting information and signals within the brain and throughout the body. i.e. **Synaptic Transmission**
![[Pasted image 20260809151239.png]]
Each neuron has anywhere between a few to hundreds of thousands of synaptic connections, and these connections can be with itself, neighboring neurons, or neurons in other regions of the brain.

## Types of Synapses
![[Pasted image 20260809151020.png]]
1. **Chemical Synapses**
	- most common type
	- involve release and reception of *neurotransmitters*,  which are chemical messengers that transmit signals from one neuron to another.
2. **Electrical Synapses**
	- also known as gap junctions
	- less common
	- unlike Chemical Synapses, they allow for direct electrical communication between neurons. Made possible by gap junctions, which are protein channels that connect the cytoplasm of adjacent neuron

## Concept of neurotransmitters and their receptors at synapses
### Neurotransmitters 
Chemical messengers that play a crucial role in communication between neurons at synapses.

They are released by the presynaptic neuron and act on specific receptors located on the postsynaptic neuron, enabling the transmission of signals across the synapse.

This process is fundamental to how the nervous system functions, as it allows neurons to relay information and coordinate various physiological and cognitive processes.

Neurotransmitters : diverse group of chemicals  --> that are Synthesized and Stored within Presynaptic neuron's vesicles ---> when action potential reaches presynaptic terminal --> it triggers the release of the neurotransmitters into the synaptic cleft. 

---> neurotransmitters then diffuse across the cleft and bind to specific receptors on the postsynaptic neuron.

### common neurotransmitters 
1. **Glutamate:** most abundant excitatory neurotransmitter in the brain, involved in promoting the generation of [[action potentials]] and enhancing synaptic transmission.
2.  **Gamma-Aminobutyric Acid (GABA):** primary inhibitory neurotransmitter in the brain, involved in reducing neuronal activity and dampening excitatory signals.
3. **Dopamine:** Plays a role in regulating motivation, reward, and pleasure, as well as motor control and various cognitive functions.
4. **Serotonin:** Regulates mood, sleep, appetite, and other behavioral and physiological processes
5. **Acetylcholine:** Involved in muscle contraction at the neuromuscular junction and also functions as a neuromodulator in the brain, affecting attention, learning, and memory.
6. **Norepinephrine (noradrenaline):** Influences arousal, alertness, and stress response, among other functions.
7. **Epinephrine (adrenaline):** Primarily involved in the "fight or flight" response and stress-related reactions.
8. **Endorphins:** Act as natural painkillers and are associated with feelings of pleasure and euphoria.
