# Neuron Structure 

<img src="neuron.png" style="float: left; width: 320px; margin-right: 20px;">
-  **Dendrites** are the region where one neuron receives connections from other neurons. They are protrusions that extend from the soma in multiple direction.
- The **cell body or soma** contains the nucleus and the other organelles necessary for cellular function.
- The **axon** is a key component of nerve cells over which information is transmitted from one part of the neuron (e.g., the cell body) to the terminal regions of the neuron. Axons can be rather long, extending up to a meter or so in some human sensory and motor nerve cells.
- The **synapse** is the terminal region of the axon and it is here where one neuron forms a connection with another and conveys information through the process of **synaptic transmission**
- One neuron can receive contacts from many different neurons. It has been estimated that one neuron can receive contacts from up to 10,000 other cells. 
- Consequently, the potential complexity of the networks is vast.  Similarly, any one neuron can contact up to 10,000 postsynaptic cells.

# History
- concept of the neuron as a distinct unit was established by the Spanish pathologist Santiago Ramón y Cajal.
- #### Theories of Camilo Golgi and Santiago Ramón y Cajal. 
	- Golgi proposed the reticular theory, suggesting that the nervous system was network-like, similar to the circulatory system. 
	- Cajal proposed the neuron doctrine, suggesting that the neuron is a single entity and that neurons are connected in a discontinuous manner. 
	- neuron doctrine was later confirmed with the advent of electron microscopy.
